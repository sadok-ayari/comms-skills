# Press release review

A release has one job: give a journalist something they can publish without checking with you first, and give your principal something they would sign again.

## Read it as three readers

1. **The journalist.** Is the news in the first sentence? If the headline and the first line do not carry a fact a reader did not know yesterday, the release is an announcement of nothing.
2. **The principal's office.** Would they be comfortable if this quoted them in a hostile interview? Every quoted sentence is a sentence they will have to defend.
3. **Legal or the counterpart's comms desk.** Does it name anyone who has not consented, promise anything not approved, or state a figure that cannot be produced?

## The checks

- **One news line.** Single sentence, in the first two lines, with the fact. Not the ambition.
- **Quote that says something.** A quote adds position, judgement or emotion. "We are delighted" is filler; cut it or get a real sentence.
- **Attribution, everywhere.** Every claim has an owner: a named person, a document, a dataset. Unattributed adjectives are the mark of a draft nobody has defended.
- **Numbers that reconcile.** The figures in the release, the figures in the underlying report, and the figures in the accompanying note are the same figures.
- **Boilerplate that is current.** The organisation description, the contact name, the phone number, the website. These rot silently.
- **Nothing invented in the edit.** Do not add a quote, a statistic, a partner name or a date that was not in the source material, however helpful it would be.

## Structure that survives editing

Headline (the fact) → first paragraph (who, what, when, where, why) → quote → supporting detail → quote from the second party if there is one → boilerplate → contact.

## Language

Plain, active, specific. No "world-class", "unique", "cutting-edge", "innovative", "state-of-the-art". If a word would survive unchanged in a competitor's release, it is not carrying information.

Bilingual releases: run the parity check before circulation. Two versions, one message.

## Output

Return the draft marked up: deletions struck, moves indicated, and a short list of what is missing and who has to supply it. Do not hand back a rewritten release unless asked; the author learns nothing and the voice changes.
