# Voice registers

Two people can write about the same programme and only one of them sounds like the institution. Voice is not style preference; it is who the reader believes is speaking. This skill builds the register card that makes that voice repeatable, by anyone, on any day.

## The register card

One card per client or counterpart. Eight fields, no prose.

```yaml
counterpart: <institution or principal>
salutation: <exact form of address, with examples>
language: <which language is authoritative; which is translated>
terms: <where the approved terminology lives>
person: <first person plural, third person, institution name>
rhythm: <target sentence length, paragraph ceiling>
banned: <words, punctuation, constructions>
numbers: <exact and sourced, rounded, or omitted>
signoff: <closing and signature block>
example: <one approved paragraph, quoted verbatim>
```

The `example` field is the one that matters. A card with no quoted paragraph is a list of preferences; with one, it is a reference.

## How to build it

1. **Collect their own words.** Three to five documents they wrote or signed: a letter, a page of their site, a speech, a press release.
2. **Extract patterns, not adjectives.** Sentence length, how they open, how they close, whether they use "we" or the institution's name, which punctuation they never use.
3. **Quote a paragraph.** Verbatim, with the source. This is the anchor every later draft is measured against.
4. **Record what is banned**, and why in five words. "Em-dashes: reads translated." "Exclamation marks: not institutional."
5. **Test it**: write one paragraph in the card's voice, then hand it to someone who knows the client and ask only "does this sound like them?"

## The five-item check, run on every draft

1. Zero banned punctuation.
2. Zero banned terms.
3. Salutation and sign-off correct.
4. Every number traced to a source.
5. Exactly one ask, in the last paragraph.

Five items, not fifty. A longer checklist gets skimmed; this one gets run.

## Sweeping an existing artefact

When handed a document to fix rather than write:

- Mark bans first (search and replace), because they are mechanical and unarguable.
- Then read for rhythm: if sentences run long, split them; if the text is clipped, join them.
- Then read the opening and closing against the card's example. Most voice failures live in the first and last paragraph.
- Leave the middle alone unless the register says otherwise. Rewriting the middle is how a document loses the author.

## Rules that keep a voice honest

- **The voice belongs to the counterpart.** If your sentence is better but theirs, it is wrong.
- **Never invent a quotation.** A quote that sounds like them but was not said is the one error nobody forgives.
- **A register is not a cage.** Revisit it when the principal changes, when an election changes the government, or when the client says "that is not us any more".
- **Hand the card over.** The register belongs to the client; the adviser keeps a copy.

## Pitfalls

- **Building it from your own past drafts.** You will encode your habits and call them theirs.
- **Ten fields where five would do.** The card is read at speed, mid-task.
- **Confusing tone with substance.** Softer words, same facts; never the reverse.
